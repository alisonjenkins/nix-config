# AI tells

A checklist for scanning a draft. Each entry is the pattern, then the fix.

## Vocabulary

Replace with plain words: additionally, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance,
fostering, garner, interplay, intricate, landscape used abstractly, pivotal,
showcase, tapestry used abstractly, testament, underscore, vibrant.

Fancy ways to say "is": serves as, stands as, boasts, features. Say is or has.

Abstract metaphor nouns that read technical but have a concrete equivalent:
substrate becomes base, wedge in becomes add, vector becomes way or method,
locus, vantage, nexus, primitive as a noun, harness as a metaphor, surface as
in "API surface", bedrock, scaffolding as a metaphor, modality, paradigm,
gold-plating becomes more than the job needs, ratchet becomes the mechanism's
real name, evacuate becomes move out, endgame becomes the last phase, north
star, flywheel.

## Content patterns

- **Puffery.** "pivotal moment", "testament to", "evolving landscape",
  "setting the stage for", "indelible mark". State what happened instead.
- **Promotional language.** "nestled", "vibrant", "breathtaking",
  "groundbreaking", "renowned", "stunning", "must-visit". Describe neutrally.
- **Superficial -ing phrases.** "highlighting...", "ensuring...",
  "reflecting...", "showcasing...". Delete, or expand into a real claim with a
  source.
- **Vague attribution.** "Experts believe", "Industry reports suggest", "Some
  critics argue". Name the source or drop the sentence.
- **Name-dropping.** A list of outlets or tools with no context. Pick one and
  say what it actually said or did.
- **Formulaic challenge framing.** "Despite challenges, X continues to
  thrive." Replace with the specific facts.
- **Generic conclusions.** "The future looks bright." State the plan or the
  number.
- **Cutoff disclaimers.** "While specific details are limited...". Find the
  detail or cut the sentence.

## Sentence patterns

- **"Not just X, but Y."** State the point directly.
- **Rule of three.** Ideas forced into groups of three. Use the natural count,
  even when it is two or four.
- **Synonym cycling.** Protagonist, main character, central figure, hero in one
  paragraph. Pick one word and repeat it.
- **False ranges.** "from X to Y" where X and Y are not on a scale. List the
  items.
- **Filler.** "in order to" becomes "to", "due to the fact that" becomes
  "because", "it is important to note that" gets deleted.
- **Hedge stacks.** "could potentially possibly be argued that it might"
  becomes "may".

## Chat artifacts

Strip from anything written down: "I hope this helps", "Let me know if...",
"Of course", "Certainly", "Great question", "You're absolutely right", "Found
the smoking gun".

Sycophancy is worst in a disagreement. A rejection that opens with praise
reads as an apology for the rejection. State what the code does, cite the
line, and let that carry it.

## Formatting

Covered in the parent skill under punctuation and formatting. The short
version: no em dashes and no hyphen or parenthesis standing in for one, colons
only before a list or example, sentence case headings, straight quotes, no
decorative emoji, no bolded label that restates the line beneath it.
